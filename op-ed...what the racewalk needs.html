USA RACEWALK Home Upcoming Results Athletes

Racewalking doesn’t lack talent, it lacks visibility.

Why does racewalking almost never appear in track and field media headlines or pre-meet coverage? This wasn’t just an issue at this past Outdoor Nationals. It’s been happening for years. For a long time, racewalking has existed on the fringes of the sport, rarely acknowledged in official posts, previews, or storytelling.

Before USA Racewalk established a presence on Instagram, there wasn’t a single platform consistently dedicated to showcasing U.S. racewalkers; not just their results, but who they are as athletes and people. That absence matters.

Who Are We Reaching?

Right now, participation in racewalking events is thin especially among athletes under 25 and those just beyond the youth level. This isn’t just a cyclical dip. The deeper issue is that racewalking, as it’s currently presented, isn’t appealing to younger generations.

The truth is, we haven’t built a culture that invites people in.

The Visibility Gap

There’s been little collective movement encouraging racewalkers to show up on social media, to tell their stories, or to make themselves visible beyond race results. Of course, there are exceptions. Emmanuel and Celina Corvera have done an incredible job showing their personalities and passions. Lauren Harris has begun sharing the balance between elite training, fueling, and teaching. Michael Mannozzi highlights his service background and discipline.

But these are exceptions—not the standard.

Change Is Good

Progress has been made. Recent leadership changes have brought positive steps—international opportunities, improved organization, and a centralized website. Those efforts matter. But if we want to reach the next generation, we can’t rely on outdated presentation or corny racewalking jokes.

This isn’t a lack of talent issue. Athletes like Lauren Harris, Jordan Crawford, Jason Cherng, Bricyn Healey, Angelina Colon, Angelica Harris, Cole Bennett, and competitors across the NAIA represent the next generation of American racewalking. Their stories deserve to be told.

As a sport, we already have the athletes. We already have the people. What we need now is the willingness to show who we are.

That’s the goal of USA Racewalk. To support U.S. racewalkers by highlighting not just performance, but personality, identity, and culture because that’s how people connect.

And honestly who cares who we are anyway? What matters is that racewalking finally gets the future it deserves.
